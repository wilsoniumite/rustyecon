# Pops

## Purpose

*Stub — to be written.*

Pops are the simulation's population model. They are the labour supply, the
consumers of goods, and the source of political and social pressure. Their
needs, incomes, and movements drive demand across all markets and determine
the human consequences of economic change.

---

## Open Questions Inherited from Production

**Pop expertise as a component.** The production system treats trained workforce
expertise as a component held in a building's component register. This raises
questions to be resolved here:

- How does expertise accumulate? Is it built via a training service good, or
  does it grow automatically as a building operates over time?
- Is expertise firm-specific (lost when the building exits) or worker-specific
  (retained by the pops and carried to new employment)?
- If worker-specific, how is it represented in the pop model — as a skill
  attribute on a pop group?
- What is the decay rate of expertise when a pop is unemployed or switches
  industries?

## Known Interactions with Other Systems

- **Production**: pops supply labour to buildings; buildings employ pop groups
  at a wage. Labour is an input to every recipe.
- **Markets**: pops are consumers; their demand for goods drives market prices.
  Pop income (wages + transfers) is the primary source of consumer demand.
- **Government**: pops pay taxes; governments transfer income to pops (Poor
  Laws, UBI, pensions). The political pressure pops exert on government is a
  future extension.
- **Financial Markets**: wealthy pop groups may own building shares and receive
  dividends. Aristocrat pop groups own land and receive land rent.

# Data notes: the Breakpoint B pre-look

Dated 2026-09-26. Branch `spine-eyeball`. Revised the same day after the R11 audit (S5.0.2).

These notes cover the series behind the eyeball sheet ([EYEBALL.md](EYEBALL.md)), series
by series: source, licence, coverage, validation, known breaks, and whether it may be
redistributed. The rules are docs/PLAN.md ruling 5 and Phase 5, R11 (primary sources,
validated; stop and report; never substitute silently; contestable constructions as
labelled grids) and ruling 6 (public data, public repository).

Nothing here is fitted.

## Population of England

### BoE A2 col B: Broadberry et al. (2015)

- **Source.** Broadberry, Campbell, Klein, Overton and van Leeuwen (2015), carried by the
  BoE file, sheet `A2. Pop of Eng & GB 1086-1870`, col B. Millions.
- **Licence.** BoE terms; third-party numbers. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** 1086–1870, annual.
- **Validation.** The Bank's current file (Last-Modified 2024-09-26) and the copy in the BNS
  deposit (2018) are identical on all 57 columns used (0 differing cells). At the 16
  benchmark years the series equals the BNS digitisation of Broadberry's Table 1.06 within
  0.0046 m (the table is rounded).
- **Known breaks.** Before 1541 the series is data only at 16 benchmark years: 1086, 1190,
  1220, 1250, 1279, 1290, 1315, 1325, 1348, 1351, 1377, 1400, 1430, 1450, 1522, 1541. Between
  them it is log-linear. 1087–1249 are formula cells; 1250–1540 are typed values that are
  still log-linear. 1348 to 1351 is a three-step geometric fall (4.814, 3.922, 3.195,
  2.603). From 1541 it is annual (the log path bends every year).
- **One series, not three.** A18 col Z is 1000 x A2 col B. A18 col K is Z to 1841, then the
  census column AA. A21 col G is A2 col B to 1700, then K/1000. The label "Wrigley (1997)"
  on A18 does not make it a second source. The census column reproduces Z over 1801–1841,
  so there is no step at 1842.
- **Used as.** Decade means. Before the 1540s these average interpolated values: hollow
  points in figure 2.

### Clark 2015 spreadsheet, col C "Pop England"

- **Source.** `England NNI - Clark - 2015.xlsx`, sheets `Annual` (rows 3–663, 1209–1869;
  the block below is decade midpoints and is excluded) and `Decadal`.
- **Licence.** None stated. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** 1209–1869. One value per decade before 1700 (a step function).
- **Validation.** The decadal sheet is the plain mean of the annual block. Byte-identical to
  the BNS deposit copy. Against Broadberry (BoE), the ratio BoE/Clark runs 0.73–1.12 in
  annual values over 1250–1540 (0.75–1.11 in decade means); the largest gap is 1359, where
  BoE is 27% below Clark. In decade means Clark is 2% to 8% above the BoE line in the
  1600s–1750s and −1% to +4% off it in the 1760s–1860s. For 1540–1580 it equals Clark (2007).
- **Known breaks.** The 1590s repeat the 1580s value (3.554 m). Steinsson's file corrects it
  to 4.164 after correspondence with Clark. Kept as printed and flagged. Black Death: 4.484
  for 1340–49, 3.545 from 1350.
- **Before 1540 it is partly inferred from the wage.** Clark (2010, section 8) says the
  population before 1540 is estimated as in Clark (2007). The BNS digitisation of Clark
  (2007) Table 9 (`clark07.xlsx`, sha256 04a41d6c…) has a column "Population implied by MPL
  (millions)" and a "'Best' population estimate from MPL and sample communities": the
  population that a labour-demand curve needs to produce the observed real wage, averaged
  with a series scaled from sample communities. Clark 2015's decadal values are 0.88–1.05
  times that best estimate over the 1250s–1490s: a revision of the same estimate. So before
  1540 this series is not independent evidence for the opposition between population and
  the wage. Broadberry et al. (2015) is built without wages. Over the 1260s–1310s
  Broadberry rises 7% and Clark 30%. From 1540 Clark follows Wrigley et al. (1997), then
  the census.

- BNS `broad_pop_hf` is the decade mean of the interpolated BoE series.

## Real wages

### Clark, `Wages 2014.xlsx`: real day wages (farm, building labourer, craftsman)

- **Source.** Clark's UC Davis data page. Sheet `Annual` cols B, D, E (nominal, pence per
  day), G (cost of living, 1860s = 100), I, J, K (real, 1860s = 100; Clark's own
  deflation). Sheet `Decadal`.
- **Licence.** None stated. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** 1209–1869 (the building labourer from 1221).
- **Validation.** Real = nominal / COL x a constant, exactly. Decadal = annual mean.
  Byte-identical to the BNS deposit copy. The nominal wages equal the GPIH 2006 vintage in
  every common year, so there is no vintage seam. The BoE carries them as A47 Z, AB, AC
  (labelled "Clark 2009"): equal.
- **Known breaks.** The real farm wage is printed as 0 in 1256, where the nominal wage is
  blank. Dropped from the annual series; the drop is named. The decadal sheet's 1250s value
  (56.92) includes the zero; without it the mean is 64.04. BNS `w_farm_10` inherits the
  error. The eyeball uses decade means of the annual values, without the zero.
- **Not after 1869.** Clark's cost of living ends in 1869. No Clark real wage exists after it.

### Clark 2015: male average wage over cost of living (our ratio)

- **Source.** `England NNI - Clark - 2015.xlsx`, `Annual` cols G (male average wage, d/day)
  and S (cost of living).
- **Construction.** 100 x G/S. Ours. Named "Clark avg/COL".
- **Validation.** Equals BoE A48 col V (A47 AF / A47 BG) except in 1848: Clark's two files
  print different 1848 costs of living (95.12 in the NNI file, 94.85 in the wages file), and
  the Bank carries 94.85. A 0.28% difference in one year. The Bank fills 15 years in
  1213–1244 (marked red); Clark's file leaves them blank, and so do we.

### Humphries–Weisdorf (2019), Table A2, cols F and I

- **Source.** Humphries and Weisdorf (2019), "Unreal wages?", *Economic Journal* 129,
  Table A2, as digitised by BNS (`hw19.xlsx`). The journal page is 403 from this machine.
- **Licence.** CC0 as deposited; the numbers are Humphries and Weisdorf's. Flagged: not
  committed.
- **Coverage.** Decades 1260s–1840s (HW label "1260-70" is read as the 1260s).
- **Validation.** Both columns share one deflator (E/F = H/I to rounding). The implied basket
  cost runs 193 d/yr (1260s) to 1,903 (1840s).
- **Col F is 1 + cash/basket by construction.** HW value an annual contract's in-kind
  benefits at the basket cost: implied benefits (col D) over the implied basket cost (E/F)
  are 0.993–1.001 in all 59 decades (`extract_family_b.py`, check V25b). So col F = (cash +
  basket) / basket, and only the cash part moves. The sheet shows the cash share, C / (E/F),
  beside col F: `cons_hw19_cash_over_basket_decade`, our arithmetic, not committed.
- **Known feature.** Col F (annual contracts) sits at 1.19–1.24 in every decade from the
  1260s to the 1360s. The cash share is 0.18–0.24 there, so about four-fifths of col F's
  level is fixed at 1 in those decades. Col F's percent changes are damped next to the
  day-wage series (1500s to 1640s: col F −21%, cash share −43%).

### Humphries–Weisdorf (2016), BoE A48 col E

- **Source.** The 2016 working-paper version, carried by the BoE file.
- **Licence.** BoE terms. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** 1265–1850, annual.
- **Validation.** The numerator is decadal; the denominator is an annual price close to
  Allen's CPI (the Bank's note adds a 5% rent adjustment). Within-decade movement is prices
  only. Against HW (2019) col F the decade ratio runs 0.907 (1840s) to 1.254 (1760s),
  median 1.040. Two versions of one construction. Both are reported, not reconciled.

### Allen's wages and CPI, via the BoE file (A47 AG, AH, AK; BH)

- **Source.** Allen's spreadsheet of wages and prices for London and southern England, as
  the BoE file carries it (labelled "Allen (2013), daily wage rates downloaded from GPIH").
- **Licence.** BoE terms over Allen's numbers; the GPIH copy states none. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** Wages 1264/1301–1954; the CPI (BH, pence per day for the respectable basket)
  1264–1913.
- **Validation.** `eyeball.py` checks the Bank's columns against the GPIH copy fetched today
  (sha256 a8d1b729…, equal to the probe pin). AG, AH, AI, AJ, AK and BH equal GPIH `Wages`
  cols C–G and `Prices` col DQ ("NEW CPI", pence) in every year, with the same spans (max
  relative difference 2.5e-16).
- **Known features.** The agricultural labourer's wage (AI) equals the building labourer's
  (AH) in every year of 1450–1499 and in 88% of 1650–1699. It is not plotted. The CPI's
  silver-price twin column in Allen's file is marked "Extended with Feinstein". The span of
  that extension was not identified.
- **Construction.** AH/BH (and AG/BH, AK/BH): a day's wage over the daily cost of one
  respectable basket. Ours. It is not Allen's welfare ratio, which scales by days worked,
  household size and rent under Allen's (2001) formula. That formula was not applied, and
  Allen's published welfare ratios were not reached (his site is a private login wall).

### BoE A48 col B: composite real consumption earnings

- **Source.** The Bank's own splice, 1900 = 100. The header says GB.
- **Licence.** BoE terms. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** 1209–2016.
- **Construction (the Bank's).** Chain-linked backwards from the modern level. Wage links:
  Clark average male wage (England) to 1750; Crafts–Mills 1750–70; Feinstein 1998
  1770–1881; Feinstein 1990 1881–1911; 1911–13; Feinstein UK 1913–63; ONS after. Price links:
  Clark COL to 1661; Schumpeter–Gilboy 1661–1750; Crafts–Mills 1750–70; Feinstein 1998
  1770–1882; Feinstein 1991 1882–1914; ONS after.
- **Validation.** B = 100 x (A47 B / A47 D) / (1900 value), exactly. Its ratio to
  Feinstein's A48 W is constant over 1770–1881 (relative spread 2.4e-15): it is Feinstein's
  growth there. Its ratio to Clark's real earnings (A48 V) is constant to 1661, then drifts
  to −20% by 1869.
- **Known breaks.** Every link date above. Geography: England before 1750, GB, then UK.

### Feinstein (1998), BoE A48 cols W and X

- **Source.** Feinstein (1998), "Pessimism perpetuated", *JEH* 58(3), as the BoE file
  carries it: W = A47 AR / BK (Feinstein earnings over Feinstein prices), X = A47 AQ / BL
  (over Allen 2007 prices), both 1835 = 100.
- **Licence.** BoE terms. Not redistributable. The article is paywalled from here.
- **Coverage.** W 1770–1882; X 1770–1869.
- **Validation.** Both recompute from A47 exactly. X/W runs 0.968–1.088; growth 1770–1869
  is x1.61 (X) against x1.50 (W). A band, not a choice. Allen's 2007 CPI (A47 BL) equals the
  BNS digitisation of Allen (2007) Appendix I exactly (100 years).

### Clark's nominal wages to 1914 (GPIH copy)

- **Source.** `England_1209-1914_(Clark).xls` on the GPIH site ("Provided by Gregory Clark,
  10 April 2006", re-formatted by Lindert), `main data sheet` cols CD–CF.
- **Licence.** None stated. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** Farm 1209–1869; craft and building labourer to 1914.
- **Validation.** Identical to `Wages 2014` in every common year.
- **Known breaks.** The building labourer steps +14.1% from 1868 to 1869 (35.49 to 40.51 d)
  and +15.5% from 1913 to 1914 (simple changes; the log differences are 0.132 and 0.144),
  and repeats values in 1905 and 1908–13. The 1869 step is in `Wages 2014` too; 1869 is the
  last year the two share. Used only in the rent-per-acre proxy (below), whose fall after
  1869 begins at that step.

## Land rents and land's share

### Clark 2015 spreadsheet: land rents, NNI, indirect taxes

- **Source.** `England NNI - Clark - 2015.xlsx`, cols J (land rents, £m), N (indirect taxes),
  O (net national income).
- **Licence.** None stated. Not redistributable.
- **Coverage.** 1209–1869 (J–O blank in 15 years of 1213–1244).
- **Constructions.** J/(O−N): the definition Clark (2010) states for Table 13. J/O: the
  plain ratio. Both ours. They differ by at most 0.014 (1800s).
- **Validation.** I + J + M + N = O and K + L = M hold exactly. The decadal sheet is the
  annual mean. BoE A9 col CW equals col O exactly.
- **Known breaks.** Land rents are one value per decade before 1670 and one per five-year
  block 1670–1841. From 1842 they are Schedule A "Lands" for England and Wales times a
  constant 0.939 (0.9386 in all 28 years 1842–1869, `eyeball.py`). The seam is a step: 37.29
  (1841) to 39.54 (1842), +6.0%. Land rents double from the 1590s to the 1600s (4.37 to
  9.05 £m). That jump sets the top of the floor-era band of land's share (0.22–0.25 in the
  1600s–1640s).
- **Not independent of the other land shares.** Before 1842 Clark 2015, Clark (2010) Table
  13 and Clark (2002) all rest on Clark's own rent series, in three vintages. From 1842
  Clark 2015, Tables 13 and 34 and our Stamp construction all rest on Schedule A "Lands".
  The five land-share constructions differ in denominators and vintages, not in sources.

### Clark (2010), Tables 13 and 34

- **Source.** Clark, "The macroeconomic aggregates for England, 1209–1869", working version
  `Macroagg2009.pdf`. Table 13 (factor shares by decade, 1200s–1860s). Table 34 (farmland
  rent share of England NDI, 1860s–2000s: Stamp's E&W land rents adjusted to England to
  1914; UK NDI scaled by population, Ireland at half weight). After 1914 Table 34's rents
  are Clark's projection (his section 25): Stamp's series moved with Feinstein's UK farm
  rents to 1944; then DEFRA land prices x an assumed 3% return x an assumed 28 m acres
  (1945–67); then DEFRA tenancy rents x 28 m acres (1968–2007). The 1910s-to-1920s step
  (0.022 to 0.007) falls on that seam. Table 34 also carries a real wage, 1860s–2000s:
  Clark (2005) England building wages over Feinstein's UK cost of living, then the RPI.
  That is the source's own splice of England wages over UK prices. Read from our visual
  entry; not plotted.
- **Licence.** None stated. Not redistributable. The BNS digitisation of Table 13 is CC0 as
  deposited (flagged).
- **Validation.** Table 13, text layer against the BNS digitisation of the published table:
  identical in all 67 decades. Table 34: double entry (text layer and page image), 0 cells
  differ. Its shares miss summing to 1 by up to 0.005 as printed.
- **Known defects.** In Table 13 the farm-share and output-per-worker columns for 1550–1860
  repeat the 1200–1510 values row for row: a copy error. Not used. The land-share column
  does not repeat. The 2015 spreadsheet's J/(O−N) sits below Table 13 after 1780 (1800s:
  0.157 against 0.177).

### Clark (2002), the two tables numbered 8

- **Source.** Clark, "Farmland rental values and agrarian history: England and Wales,
  1500–1912", working version `rentereh.pdf` ("forthcoming ERoEH Dec 2002"). Not the
  published article. Table 8 (rent per acre by region, £/acre, 1500–1912) and the second
  Table 8 (net agricultural output, including "Total land rents and local taxes", £m).
- **Licence.** None stated. Not redistributable. Our hand entries stay outside the repository.
- **Validation.** Double entry by two families. Family B: text layer against a hand-typed
  entry, 0 differences in both tables. Family C: text layer against its own image entry,
  0 differences, and 0 differences from family B's entries.
- **Known defects.** Table 8 prints "1800-84" between 1795–99 and 1805–09; read as 1800–04.
  In the second Table 8, output exceeds wages + rents + capital by up to £4.5m after the
  mid-1600s. That gap tracks Table 7's tax burden as a share of rents. So the column
  labelled "rents and local taxes" reads arithmetically as rent plus tithe without local
  taxes. Label and arithmetic disagree. Unresolved; the column is used as printed.
- **Periods.** 40, 20, 10 and 5 years long, as printed. Drawn over their own years.

### Our construction: Clark (2002) rents over BoE England GDP

- **Construction.** The second Table 8's rents column over the period mean of BoE A21 col D
  (England nominal GDP at factor cost). 1700–1912. Families B and C built it independently;
  the two agree to 4e-11.
- **Known issues.** The numerator's label question (above). The denominator is GDP, not NNI.
  BoE England GDP for 1700–1910 is England's share of GB (A9 CU: 86.7% in 1700, 84.8% in
  1800, 84.7% in 1900, interpolated) times GB GDP. BoE England GDP over Clark NNI swings
  0.86–1.31 (1.31 in 1800).

### Stamp (1916), Table A4: Schedule A "Lands"

- **Source.** Stamp, *British Incomes and Property* (1916), Table A4, p. 49: Income Tax
  Schedule A, Lands (including tithes), £000, England and Wales, Scotland, Ireland, UK,
  1842–3 to 1913–14. archive.org scan (University of Toronto copy). Clark cites Stamp for his
  land rents from 1842.
- **Licence.** Public domain (Stamp died 1941; archive.org marks it not in copyright).
  Redistributable. Our image entry is committed: `data/spine/stamp1916_a4_lands_entryB_visual.csv`.
- **Validation.** OCR text layer against an entry read off the page image at 300 dpi: 68 of
  72 years parse from the OCR; 2 cells differ, both resolved from the image and the row sum;
  4 years are image-only, checked by the row sum. E&W + Scotland + Ireland = UK holds
  exactly in 61 of 72 years and within £2,000 in the rest (Stamp's rounding). Stamp prints
  error margins to 1871–2. Clark 2015 land rents equal 0.939 x Stamp E&W in every year
  1842–1869.
- **Construction.** (E&W + Scotland) / BoE A21 col M (GB nominal GDP at market prices).
  Ours. Assessed gross annual value, revalued periodically. "Lands" includes tithe,
  farmhouses, woodland and building land, so it is not farmland rent alone. Clark's rents
  from 1842 are this series, so the Stamp construction is not a second witness to Clark's.

### BoE A17 col AM over col AR: rent of land and buildings

- **Source.** Mitchell (1988), ch. XVI, table 5, "Rent" (after Feinstein 1972), and GDP(I)
  at factor cost, as the BoE file carries them. UK. 1855–1920.
- **Licence.** BoE terms. Not redistributable.
- **Construction.** AM/AR. Ours. An upper bound on land's share, not a land share: it
  includes buildings. 0.150 (1855), 0.117 (1913), 0.048 (1920). The 1913–1920 fall is not
  examined. The Bank's "capital income from dwellings" (AQ) is a fixed 0.6278 x AM, so rent
  less dwellings is not an independent series and is not built.

### Clark (2023), `GDP-Efficiency 2023.xlsx` (cross-check only)

- **Source.** A workbook Clark shared privately with BNS (2023-02-18), deposited in their
  archive. Third-party. Not redistributable.
- **What it shows.** From 1500 its land rents equal the 2015 spreadsheet's in every year
  (though labelled "s./acre"). Before 1500 its rents are lower (ratio 0.27–1.04). Its land
  share of NDP is 0.071–0.108 in the 1250s–1290s, against 0.155–0.197 in the 2015 file.
  Over the 1250s–1740s its decade means run 0.071–0.249; its 1860s value, 0.075, is inside
  that range. So under this vintage land's share falls from 0.204 (1770s) to 0.075 (1860s)
  but does not leave its floor-era range before the file ends. Clark revised medieval rents
  after 2015. Used only as a named alternative in EYEBALL.md §3 and §4.

### Our construction: rent per acre in days of the day wage

- **Construction.** 240 x Clark (2002) England rent and tithe per acre (£) over the period
  mean of Clark's building labourer's day wage (pence; GPIH copy, 1500–1912) or farm
  labourer's day wage (`Wages 2014`, 1500–1869). Days of wage per acre-year. A price proxy
  for land against labour. Ours.
- **Known breaks.** Clark's rents include the tithe paid by the tenant. His England averages
